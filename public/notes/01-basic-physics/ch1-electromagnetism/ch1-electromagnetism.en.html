PHY · ElectromagnetismCharge & Coulomb

1Electric Charge & Coulomb's Law

MCQ + Written
Definition Charge (q) is the property of matter that makes it experience an electric force. It is a scalar; only two kinds exist, positive (+) and negative (−), and the SI unit is the coulomb (C) = 1 A·s.
sign first, then magnitude!
  • Quantised: q = ±ne, e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C — no fractional charge
  • Conserved: total charge of an isolated system never changes; rubbing only transfers it
  • Invariant: the same in every inertial frame (unlike relativistic mass)
Coulomb's law (1785) F = k q₁q₂ / r²
k = 1/4πε₀ = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²
ε₀ = 8.854 × 10⁻¹² C²/(N·m²)
F in newton, r in metre · attractive if unlike
You change…Force becomesReason
r → 2rF/4inverse-square law
r → r/24Fsame law, read backwards
both charges ×24FF ∝ q₁q₂
one charge ×22FF ∝ q, not r

In a medium F = Fvacuum/εr; water (εr ≈ 80) cuts the force about 80 times.

Memory trick Coulomb is central and inverse-square. Memorise k = 9 × 10⁹, not ε₀: every exam numeric uses 9 × 10⁹.
Worked example 1 ✍ +2 μC and +3 μC, 0.3 m apart, vacuum:
F = 9 × 10⁹ × 6 × 10⁻¹² / 0.09 = 0.6 N, repulsive (both positive).
Exam trap k = 9 × 10⁹ is 1/4πε₀, not ε₀. Write F = 9 × 10⁹ q₁q₂ ε₀/r² and the answer is out by ~10¹⁰.

PHY · ElectromagnetismE field & Gauss

2Electric Field (E)

high-yield
Definition The force per unit positive test charge: E = F / q₀. E is a vector, SI unit N/C = V/m; for a point charge E = kq/r².
  • Lines leave +, land on −; density ∝ E
  • They never cross — E has one direction per point
  • They meet a conductor at 90°; E = 0 inside it
  • Analogy: E = F/q ↔ g = F/m

3Gauss's Law

most tested
Statement (1835) The total electric flux through any closed surface = enclosed charge ÷ ε₀:  ∮ E·dA = qenc / ε₀ (flux ΦE = EA cos θ, unit N·m²/C).
  • Flux depends only on the charge inside — not on the size, shape or position of the surface
  • Charge outside → net flux 0: as many lines enter as leave
  • E leaves the integral easily only for symmetric distributions, where E is constant and ⟂ dA
Charge distributionField ERemember
Point charge / outside a spherekq/r²acts as if all q were at the centre
Infinite line charge (λ C/m)λ / 2πε₀r∝ 1/r, falls off slowly
Infinite plane sheet (σ C/m²)σ / 2ε₀same at every distance
Just outside a conductorσ / ε₀twice the sheet value
Inside a conductor0charges migrate to the surface
Memory trick Sphere → 1/r², line → 1/r, sheet → constant. Count the dimensions you can spread a charge over: 2, 1, 0.
Classic MCQ A charge q at the centre of a cube → flux through one face = q/6ε₀: the total q/ε₀ is shared equally by 6 faces.
Worked example ✍ E at 20 cm from a 2 μC charge: E = 9 × 10⁹ × 2 × 10⁻⁶ / 0.04 = 4.5 × 10⁵ N/C, directed away from the charge (q > 0).

4Electric Potential (V)

MCQ + Written
Definition Work done per unit positive charge in bringing a test charge from infinity: V = W/q. V is a scalar, unit volt = J/C; for a point charge V = kq/r.
Field EPotential V
naturevectorscalar
point charge qkq/r²kq/r
unitN/C = V/mvolt = J/C
relationE = −dV/dr
  • E points from high to low V
  • ΔU = qΔV; 1 eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
  • Equipotentials ⟂ field lines; no work along one
Worked example ✍ V at 30 cm from a +2 μC charge: V = 9 × 10⁹ × 2 × 10⁻⁶ / 0.3 = 6 × 10⁴ V. Doubling r halves V, while the field would fall to a quarter.

5Capacitance & Capacitors

IT favourite
Definition C = Q/V: charge stored per unit potential difference, unit farad (F) with 1 F = 1 C/V. Parallel plates: C = ε₀εrA/d.
Two capacitors C₁, C₂Result
In seriesC = C₁C₂/(C₁+C₂)
In parallelC = C₁ + C₂
Energy stored½CV² = ½Q²/C = ½QV
Charging to Vq = CV(1 − e−t/RC) · τ = RC
Memory trick Parallel adds like sums of money; series is the tightest link — limited by the smallest capacitance, exactly like resistors.
Exam trap Farad = capacitance, weber = flux, henry = inductance. Two 3 μF in series give 1.5 μF, not 6 μF.
Worked example ✍ Air plates, A = 0.01 m², d = 1 mm: C = 88.5 pF; at 200 V, Q = CV = 1.77 × 10⁻⁸ C, U = ½CV² = 0.035 J.

6Current, Resistance & Ohm's Law

MCQ + Written
Definitions Current I = Q/t (ampere, 1 A = 1 C/s) · Ohm's law V = IR · Resistance R = ρL/A, where ρ is the resistivity of the material and R a property of that piece of wire.
  • L ×2 → R doubles; A ×2 → R halves; conductivity σ = 1/ρ
  • Temperature: Rt = R₀(1 + αt), α quoted at 20 °C
  • Joule heat H = I²Rt = V²t/R; power P = VI = I²R = V²/R
Materialρ (Ω·m), 20 °Cα (°C⁻¹)
Silver (best conductor)1.59 × 10⁻⁸3.8 × 10⁻³
Copper1.68 × 10⁻⁸3.93 × 10⁻³
Aluminium2.82 × 10⁻⁸3.9 × 10⁻³
Constantan (≈ 0 alloy)4.9 × 10⁻⁷≈ 0
Nichrome (heater wire)1.0 × 10⁻⁶4 × 10⁻⁴
Worked example ✍ Copper wire, L = 100 m, A = 2 mm²: R = ρL/A = (1.68 × 10⁻⁸ × 100)/(2 × 10⁻⁶) = 0.84 Ω. At 5 A, P = I²R = 25 × 0.84 = 21 W of heat. Half L and double A → R unchanged.
Memory trick ρ = RA/L is the material fingerprint: copper 1.68 × 10⁻⁸ and silver 1.59 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m settle every "better conductor" MCQ.
Exam trap R is not a property of the material alone. Constantan and nichrome are chosen for heaters because α ≈ 0, so R stays steady as it heats.
IT link V = IR for a fixed resistance is a straight line through the origin — which is exactly why a two-resistor voltage divider scales a 5 V logic level, and how every SPICE resistor model works. Ohm's law is the only ingredient in Kirchhoff's laws.

7Magnetic Field (B) & Its Sources

MCQ + Written
Definition The region in which a moving charge or a current feels a force. B is a vector, SI unit tesla (T) = N/(A·m) = Wb/m², and 1 T = 10⁴ gauss (1 G = 10⁻⁴ T). μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A.

Biot–Savart law for a small current element: dB = (μ₀/4π) · I dl sin θ / r²

SourceBFalls off as
Long straight wireμ₀I / 2πr1/r
Centre of a circular loopμ₀I / 2R1/R (not 1/R²)
Solenoid, inside (n turns/m)μ₀nIuniform, ∝ n
Long solenoid, far outsideμ₀NI / 2Llike one wire, 1/r
  • Straight wire: thumb = conventional current, curled fingers = B
  • Solenoid: fingers follow the winding, thumb points north
  • Like currents in parallel wires attract, opposite currents repel
Forces on charges and wires Lorentz force F = q(v × B), |F| = qvB sin θ
Wire F = BIL sin θ · coil torque τ = NIAB sin θ
Circular path r = mv/qB · ωc = qB/m
Memory trick Fleming's left hand = motor: Field, seCond, thuMb. The right hand is the generator rule.
Exam trap F = 0 when θ = 0° (v ∥ B) or the charge rests, and the magnetic force does no work — it bends the path, never the speed.
Worked example 1 ✍ B at 5 cm from a long straight wire carrying 2 A: B = 2 × 10⁻⁷ × 2 / 0.05 = 8 × 10⁻⁶ T, anticlockwise for upward current.
Worked example 2 ✍ Solenoid n = 500 turns/m, I = 2 A: B = 4π × 10⁻⁷ × 500 × 2 = 1.26 × 10⁻³ T inside. An electron at 10⁶ m/s feels F = qvB = 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁶ N.

8Magnetic Flux & Faraday's Law

most tested
Magnetic flux ΦB = BA cos θ, unit weber (Wb) = T·m² = V·s. θ is the angle between B and the area normal (0° gives maximum flux, 90° none). Faraday (1831): a changing magnetic flux induces an emf, ε = −N dΦB/dt for N turns.

Lenz's law is the minus sign: the induced current always opposes the change in flux that produced it, so no energy is created — this is conservation of energy.

An emf appears when B, the area or θ changes, and also when the circuit moves. A steady flux, even a huge one, induces nothing.

SituationInduced emfNote
N-turn coil, flux changeε = −N ΔΦ/ΔtN multiplies the emf
Rod of length L moving at v ⟂ Bε = BLvmotional; no magnet needed
Coil rotating at ω in Bε = NBAω sin ωtalternating, εmax = NBAω
Self-inductanceε = −L dI/dtopposes a change of current
Mutual inductanceε₂ = −M dI₁/dtbasis of the transformer
Memory trick Faraday in words: more turns, faster change, bigger emf. Lenz in words: the induced effect always brakes the cause.
Exam trap Lenz opposes the change, not the field. Push a magnet in and the coil repels; pull it out and it still attracts. An emf is volts, not newtons.
Worked example ✍ The flux through a 100-turn coil falls from 0.05 Wb to 0.01 Wb in 0.2 s:
|ε| = N ΔΦ/Δt = 100 × (0.05 − 0.01)/0.2 = 100 × 0.04/0.2 = 20 V, in the direction that opposes the flux collapsing.
IT link Transformers need AC only — a steady DC flux never induces anything, which is why switching-mode PSUs rectify to DC and then chop it back to a changing current before the transformer sees it. The same Faraday law reads a card mag-strip and writes a hard disk.

9Self & Mutual Inductance

MCQ + Written
Definition Self-inductance: a coil opposes any change of its own current — L = NΦB/I, emf = −L dI/dt, energy U = ½LI². Mutual: a changing current in coil 1 induces an emf in coil 2 — M = N₂Φ₂/I₁, ε₂ = −M dI₁/dt. Unit: henry (H) = V·s/A.
  • L ∝ N²; a soft-iron core raises it enormously
  • A current cannot change instantly in an inductor
  • Transformer: Vs/Vp = Ns/Np, Is/Ip = Np/Ns

10Eddy Currents

Definition A bulk conductor cutting a changing magnetic flux develops closed circulating currents inside the material. Lenz's law makes them oppose the motion, so the motion is damped and the energy emerges as Joule heat (I²Rt).
  • Flux must change: a solid block heats the moment it moves
  • Uses: induction furnace, eddy-current brake, metal detector, induction cooker, meter damping
  • Core losses are killed by laminated silicon-steel laminations
Exam trap Eddy currents are not a single current along a path — every closed loop inside the metal carries one, and all of them oppose the motion: drag, never push.
Worked example ✍ A 2 H inductor carries 3 A: U = ½LI² = ½ × 2 × 9 = 9 J. Cutting the current to zero in 0.05 s gives an average opposing emf LΔI/Δt = 2 × 3/0.05 = 120 V — the reason a switch across an inductive load sparks.

11Maxwell's Equations (1865)

most tested

Four equations that unify electricity, magnetism and light. Maxwell predicted EM waves in 1865; Hertz produced and detected them in 1887–88.

#LawIntegral formSays
1Gauss (E)∮ E·dA = q/ε₀charge is the source of E
2Gauss (B)∮ B·dA = 0no magnetic monopoles
3Faraday∮ E·dl = −dΦB/dtchanging B creates a circulating E
4Ampère–Maxwell∮ B·dl = μ₀I + μ₀ε₀ dΦE/dtcurrent and changing E create B

Differential: ∇·E = ρ/ε₀ · ∇·B = 0 · ∇×E = −∂B/∂t · ∇×B = μ₀J + μ₀ε₀ ∂E/∂t

Displacement current Id = ε₀ dΦE/dt — Maxwell's own addition. It lets "current" count as flowing across the gap of a charging capacitor, and it makes EM waves possible.
Memory trick Order G–G–F–A: two divergences (sources — charge: yes · monopole: no) and two curls (circulation — Faraday, Ampère).

12Electromagnetic Waves

  • Speed in vacuum c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀) = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, independent of frame or source
  • E ⟂ B ⟂ direction of travel; |E| = cB, so E/B = c
  • Transverse, self-sustaining, needs no medium
  • Carries energy (S = E×B/μ₀) and momentum
  • The whole spectrum shares c: c = fλ
Worked example ✍ At 100 MHz, λ = 3 × 10⁸ / 10⁸ = 3 m (FM band). A 60 Hz mains line has λ = 5 × 10⁶ m — five thousand kilometres per cycle.
Exam trap It is the second equation (∇·B = 0) that rules out monopoles. ∇·E = ρ/ε₀ uses the volume density ρ; the integral form uses the total charge q inside.
IT link Free-space wave impedance Z₀ = √(μ₀/ε₀) ≈ 377 Ω (≈ 120π Ω), and a half-wave dipole radiates about 73 Ω — exactly why Wi-Fi antennas run on 75 Ω coax.
