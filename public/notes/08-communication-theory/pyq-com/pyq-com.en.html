COM · Solved Past QuestionsMultiplexing II: types, FDM
Q2 Differentiate among TDM, FDM and WDM. How does synchronous TDM work? Write about synchronous and statistical TDM.Combined Bank SO(IT) 2023 · RAKUB ANSE 2023
Synchronous TDM: every input owns a fixed slot in every frame, whether it has data or not. Frame = one slot per input (+ framing bits). Output rate = n × input rate. Empty slot = wasted capacity.
Statistical TDM: slots are given only to inputs with data, so each slot carries an address. No fixed frame; link rate can be below n × input rate. Used by packet networks.
Exam trap "FDM is for analog, TDM for digital" is the standard line, but FDM can carry modulated digital data too (Wi-Fi OFDM). The 49th BCS MCQ asks TDM's main disadvantage over FDM: complex synchronisation.
Q3 What is multiplexing? Describe the different types of multiplexing, with examples, and explain time-division multiplexing in detail.Combined Bank AME/AHE 2023 · SPCBL AME 2021 · SPCBL SAME 2022
  1. FDM: each signal modulates its own carrier; bands are separated by guard bands (radio, TV, 1G AMPS).
  2. WDM: several light wavelengths on one fibre, combined by a prism / grating (DWDM: 40–160 λ).
  3. TDM: synchronous (fixed slot per input) and statistical (slot on demand, with an address). Interleaving unit = bit, byte or block; a framing bit marks each frame.
  4. CDM / CDMA: every user sends in the whole band at the same time, separated by orthogonal codes (3G).

5The sampling theorem

38th, 46th BCS
Nyquist sampling theorem A signal band-limited to fmax is fully recovered from its samples if fs ≥ 2fmax. Nyquist rate = 2fmax; Nyquist interval = 1/(2fmax). Sampling slower causes aliasing: high frequencies fold back as false low ones.
Q19 Determine the Nyquist sampling rate and the Nyquist sampling interval for x(t) = sin(2100πt).38th BCS (2018)
  1. Compare with sin(2πft): 2πf = 2100π → fmax = 1050 Hz
  2. Nyquist rate = 2 × 1050 = 2100 samples/s
  3. Nyquist interval = 1/2100 s ≈ 0.476 ms (476 µs)
Exam trap The coefficient of t is ω = 2πf, not f. Students write f = 2100 Hz and get 4200 samples/s. Some printed answers write "f = d/2" without a result: always finish with the number and the unit.

6Channel capacity: two formulas

13 exams
Nyquist (noiseless) R = 2B log₂L
B = bandwidth, L = signal levels. More levels → more bits, until noise stops you.
Shannon (noisy) C = B log₂(1 + SNR)
SNR as a ratio: SNR = 10dB/10. The ceiling no coding can beat.

Q30 What is 3 dB? How can you turn a converter into an inverter?BTRC AD(Technical) 2019

3 dB = a power ratio of 2 (10 log 2 = 3.01): −3 dB is half power (0.707 of voltage). The 3-dB bandwidth is the band between the two half-power points of a filter or amplifier. Converter → inverter: a fully controlled (thyristor) rectifier works as a line-commutated inverter when its firing angle is above 90° and a DC source of the right polarity drives it: power then flows from DC to the AC side.

8Modulation and AM

15 exams
Q32 What is modulation? Why is it necessary? What are modulation and demodulation? Write five uses.BPSC AE 2022 · BCSCL NE 2017
Definition Modulation varies a property (amplitude, frequency or phase) of a high-frequency carrier in step with the message. Demodulation recovers the message at the receiver.
  1. Practical antenna size: length ≈ λ/4; 3 kHz audio needs a 25-km antenna, 100 MHz needs 75 cm
  2. Multiplexing: different carriers share one medium (FDM)
  3. Range: high frequencies radiate and travel further
  4. Noise: FM / digital schemes resist noise
  5. Matching: shift the signal into the band the channel passes
Uses: ① AM/FM radio broadcast ② TV ③ mobile phones (GMSK, QAM) ④ modems / DSL ⑤ Wi-Fi, satellite and radar links.
Memory trick Why modulate? "Antenna, Multiplex, Range, Noise" = AMRN.

11Pulse code modulation

38th BCS · BTCL

Q43 Explain the concept of entropy in information theory. How does it quantify the uncertainty of a random message source?47th BCS ICT
Definition The information in a symbol of probability p is I = log₂(1/p) bits: rare symbols carry more. Entropy is the average information per symbol: H = −Σ pᵢ log₂ pᵢ bits/symbol. It is largest (log₂M) when all M symbols are equally likely (most uncertain) and 0 when one symbol is certain. Information rate R = r × H (r = symbols per second).

Q48 Differentiate low-pass and band-pass channels with examples.15th NTRCA ICT

Low-pass: passes 0 to f₁ (starts at zero) → baseband digital signals go straight on it (a LAN cable). Band-pass: passes f₁ to f₂, not starting at zero → the signal must be modulated to fit (a radio or telephone voice channel, 300–3400 Hz). A low-pass channel is usually one wide medium used by one pair of devices; a band-pass channel is a slice of a shared medium.

Q49 What is the main benefit of a broadband transmission system compared with baseband?Rupali Bank ANA 2023

Baseband puts one digital signal on the whole bandwidth of the cable (Ethernet). Broadband modulates signals onto separate carriers, so many channels travel at once (FDM: cable TV, DSL, cable Internet) and over longer distances with amplifiers.

Q50 What is channel hardening, and what does it imply for frequency allocation (in OFDMA, for example)?BTRC DAD(Technical) 2019

In massive MIMO, with many base-station antennas the fading on the individual paths averages out, so the effective channel gain of each user becomes almost constant (deterministic) over time and frequency. Then OFDMA need not chase the best sub-carriers per user (frequency-selective scheduling); every user can get the whole band, with fewer pilots and simpler power control.

Exam trap λ = c/f: kHz → hundreds of metres, MHz → metres, GHz → centimetres. A wavelength of 476 km for a broadcast station signals a unit slip.

The problem with synchronous mode: sender and receiver must keep their clocks locked for the whole block; a small drift over thousands of bits misplaces every later bit, and the receiver must buffer the block. The asynchronous fix: each character brings its own start bit, so the receiver re-locks every 10 bits; drift never accumulates, at the cost of the start/stop overhead.

Q56 Explain the structure and characteristics of fibre-optic cable, its advantages and disadvantages, and how data travels through it.17th NTRCA ICT (2023) · 13th NTRCA CSE (2016)
Structure: glass core (9 µm single-mode, 50/62.5 µm multimode), cladding with a lower refractive index, buffer coating, strength members (Kevlar), outer jacket.
