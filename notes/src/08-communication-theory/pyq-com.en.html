COM · Past QuestionsShannon & SNR

1Shannon–Hartley Channel Capacity

asked 5×
Formula For a noisy channel the maximum error-free rate is C = B log₂(1 + S/N) bits/s. B = bandwidth in Hz, S/N = linear power ratio. If SNR is given in dB, first convert: S/N = 10^(SNRdB/10).
Q1 What is the channel capacity for a teleprinter channel with a 300 Hz bandwidth and a signal-to-noise ratio of 3 dB?
MRA AME 2022
  1. dB → ratio: S/N = 10^(3/10) = 10^0.3 = 1.995
  2. log₂ 2.995 = 3.32 × log₁₀ 2.995 = 1.583
  3. C = 300 × 1.583 = ≈ 475 bps
Q2 A telephone line normally has a bandwidth of 3000 Hz (300 to 3300 Hz) assigned for data communications. The signal-to-noise ratio is usually 3162. Calculate the capacity of this channel.
RPGCL AM(ICT) 2022 (BUET) · asked twice
  1. 3162 is a ratio (≈ 35 dB), so use it directly
  2. C = 3000 × log₂(1 + 3162) = 3000 × log₂ 3163 = 3000 × 11.627 = ≈ 34,881 bps ≈ 34.9 kbps
MCQ Channel capacity of a band-limited Gaussian channel with SNR = 15 and bandwidth 16 kHz? (a) 32 (b) 64 (c) 18 (d) 128 kbps · 49th BCS 2025

C = 16,000 × log₂(1 + 15) = 16,000 × 4 = 64 kbps → (b).

3Nyquist Bit Rate (Noiseless Channel)

written
Formula On a noiseless channel of bandwidth B using L signal levels: BitRate = 2 × B × log₂ L bps (log₂ L bits per level, at most 2B symbols/s).
Q4 We need to send 265 kbps over a noiseless channel with a bandwidth of 20 kHz. How many signal levels do we need?
asked in a recruitment paper
  1. 265,000 = 2 × 20,000 × log₂ L → log₂ L = 6.625 → L = 2^6.625 ≈ 98.7
  2. L must be a power of 2 → 128 levels (7 bits): 2 × 20,000 × 7 = 280 kbps ≥ 265 ✓ (or 64 levels at 240 kbps)

Channelnoiselessnoisy
Formula2B log₂ LB log₂(1 + S/N)
Answersrate for a chosen L (or L for a rate)the upper limit, whatever L

Use both: B = 1 MHz, S/N = 63 → C = 6 Mbps (ceiling); choose 4 Mbps → 2 × 1M × log₂ L → L = 4.

4Nyquist Sampling Rate

38th BCS
Q5 Determine the Nyquist sampling rate and the Nyquist sampling interval for the signal x(t) = sin(2100πt).
38th BCS Written · 2018
  1. Compare with sin(2πft): 2πf = 2100π → fmax = 1050 Hz
  2. Nyquist rate fs = 2fmax = 2100 samples/s
  3. Nyquist interval Ts = 1/fs = 1/2100 s = 0.476 ms

Sampling theorem: a signal band-limited to fmax is recovered exactly from samples taken at fs ≥ 2fmax. Below that, spectrum copies overlap: aliasing. Cure: anti-aliasing LPF + slight oversampling (voice 3.4 kHz → 8 kHz).

Exam trap 2100 in sin(2100πt) is 2f, not f: the rate is 2100 Hz, not 4200 Hz; never "f/2".

45th BCS Written (CSE-971) · 31.01.2024
Multiplexing Sending several signals over one shared link at the same time. A MUX at the sender combines n inputs; a DEMUX at the receiver separates them. Types: FDM and WDM (analog, by frequency / wavelength) and TDM (digital, by time: synchronous or statistical).

MCQ Main disadvantage of TDM compared with FDM? (a) higher bandwidth (b) noise immunity (c) complex synchronisation (d) limited frequency usage · 49th BCS 2025

Answer (c): every slot must be found at the right instant, so TDM needs framing bits and tight clock sync. FDM inputs run continuously in their own band.

TDM in one line Several signals share one channel by dividing its time, not its frequency; WDM is FDM for light in a fibre.

6Guard Bands & FDM Bandwidth

numerical
Guard band A small unused frequency range between adjacent channels, so their spectra do not overlap. N channels of bandwidth B with guard G need N − 1 guard bands: BW = NB + (N − 1)G
Q7 Five channels, each with a 100-kHz bandwidth, are to be multiplexed together. What is the minimum bandwidth of the link if there is a need for a guard band of 10 kHz between the channels to prevent interference?
Combined Bank SO(IT) (E-zone) · 25.07.2026

N = 5, B = 100 kHz, G = 10 kHz → BW = 5 × 100 + (5 − 1) × 10 = 500 + 40 = 540 kHz

Q8 Solve the given numerical problem on Frequency Division Multiplexing (FDM). Show all necessary calculations and determine the required frequency allocation / bandwidth.
Bangladesh Satellite Company PLC AE (CSE) · 21.08.2026 · the data vary; a typical version below
Worked example ✍ Four voice channels of 4 kHz each, guard band 1 kHz, link starting at 60 kHz. BW = 4 × 4 + 3 × 1 = 19 kHz → link 60–79 kHz. Method: place the channels in order, adding B for each channel and G between neighbours.

7TDM & Source-Rate Numericals

Synchronous TDM ✍ Four 1-kbps inputs, 1-bit slots: 1000 frames/s, 4 bits/frame, link = 4 × 1 = 4 kbps, bit time 250 μs. E1 = 32 × 8 × 8000 = 2.048 Mbps; T1 = (24 × 8 + 1) × 8000 = 1.544 Mbps (1 framing bit).
Q9 A digitized TV picture uses a matrix of 480 × 500 pixels; each pixel can take one of 32 intensity values; 30 pictures are sent per second. Find the source rate R (bps).
Bangladesh Bank AME (BIBM) · 04.02.2023

Pixels = 480 × 500 = 240,000 · bits/pixel = log₂ 32 = 5 · R = 240,000 × 5 × 30 = 36,000,000 bps = 36 Mbps

Memory trick Every rate is (things per second) × (bits per thing): pixels × bits × frames; samples × bits; slots × bits × frames.

8Pulse Code Modulation (PCM)

38th BCS
Q10 What is modulation? Describe the pulse code modulation (PCM) technique. State the advantages and disadvantages of PCM.
38th BCS Written · 2018
Modulation Varying a property (amplitude, frequency or phase) of a high-frequency carrier in step with the message. Needed for practical antenna size (≈ λ/4), long range, and multiplexing many users.
PCM Converts an analog signal into a stream of binary code words: the signal is sampled, each sample is quantized to one of L = 2ⁿ levels, and each level is sent as an n-bit code.
  1. LPF band-limits the message to fm (anti-aliasing). Sampler takes samples at fs ≥ 2fm (PAM pulses).
  2. Quantizer rounds each sample to the nearest of L = 2ⁿ levels (error ≤ ±Δ/2 = quantization noise). Encoder turns each level into an n-bit word.
  3. Regenerative repeaters re-create clean pulses, so noise does not pile up. Receiver: regenerate → decode → reconstruction LPF.
Formulas L = 2ⁿ · bit rate Rb = n fs · min. bandwidth = n fs/2 · SQNR ≈ 6.02n + 1.76 dB (+6 dB per extra bit) · voice: 8 bits × 8 kHz = 64 kbps (DS0)
Advantages
  • high noise immunity; repeaters regenerate
  • good for long distance
  • easy encryption, error coding, TDM
  • all media share one digital format
Disadvantages
  • large bandwidth (64 kbps vs a 4 kHz voice band)
  • quantization noise
  • complex encoder/decoder circuits
  • needs bit and frame synchronisation

9Delta Modulation (DM)

MCQ
MCQ Which suffers higher slope-overload and granular noise? (a) DPCM (b) DM (c) ADM (d) PCM · 49th BCS 2025

Answer (b) DM: 1 bit per sample, fixed step Δ. Slope overload when the signal rises faster than Δ·fs; granular noise when the signal is flat and the staircase hunts ±Δ. ADM adapts Δ; DPCM sends multi-bit differences.

Exam trap PCM output is a train of binary code words; pulses whose width follows the signal are PWM, not PCM.

10AM Bandwidth

MCQ
MCQ Carrier 1 MHz, modulating signal 10 kHz: bandwidth of the AM signal? (a) 5 kHz (b) 10 kHz (c) 20 kHz (d) 2 MHz · 49th BCS 2025

DSB-AM puts sidebands at fc ± fm = 990 and 1010 kHz → BW = 2fm = 20 kHz (c). The carrier frequency does not enter.

Also: m = Am/Ac · Pt = Pc(1 + m²/2) → at m = 1 only 1/3 of the power is in the sidebands · SSB needs only fm.

11FM: Deviation, Index, Carson Bandwidth

written
Q11 A sinusoidal modulating waveform of amplitude 5 V and frequency 2 kHz is applied to an FM generator which has a frequency sensitivity of 40 Hz/volt. Calculate the frequency deviation, modulation index and bandwidth.
Bangladesh Ordnance Factory AP 2022 (MIST)
  1. Deviation Δf = kf × Am = 40 Hz/V × 5 V = 200 Hz
  2. Modulation index β = Δf / fm = 200 / 2000 = 0.1 (no unit)
  3. Carson's rule: BW = 2(Δf + fm) = 2(200 + 2000) = 4400 Hz = 4.4 kHz
  4. Remark: β < 0.3 → narrowband FM, BW ≈ 2fm = 4 kHz; Carson's 4.4 kHz is the safe answer

13Digital Modulation: ASK, FSK, PSK, QPSK, QAM

asked 4×
Digital-to-analog Binary data change one property of a sine carrier: ASK the amplitude, FSK the frequency, PSK the phase. QPSK uses four phases (2 bits/symbol). QAM changes amplitude and phase together (16-QAM: 4 bits/symbol).

Rules bits/symbol n = log₂ M · baud = bit rate / n · symbol time Ts = n × Tb · ideal bandwidth efficiency ≈ n bit/s/Hz
MCQ At the same bit rate, QPSK symbol time TQ vs BPSK symbol time TB? (a) 0.25TB (b) 4TB (c) 0.5TB (d) 2TB · 49th BCS 2025

BPSK: 1 bit/symbol → TB = Tb. QPSK: 2 bits/symbol → one symbol lasts two bit times → TQ = 2TB (d); half the baud, half the bandwidth.

MCQ Most bandwidth-efficient? (a) BPSK (b) QPSK (c) FSK (d) 16QAM · MCQ Modulation in GSM? (a) MSK (b) FSK (c) ASK (d) GMSK · both 49th BCS 2025

16QAM (d): 4 bits/symbol vs 1–2 for the others. GMSK (d): MSK shaped by a Gaussian filter (BT = 0.3); constant envelope lets the handset use an efficient non-linear amplifier and keeps the spectrum inside a 200 kHz carrier (270.833 kbps).

Exam trap More bits per symbol → longer symbol time, not shorter. Higher-order QAM saves bandwidth but needs a higher SNR.
Memory trick "QAM = Quantity And Mood": amplitude (how loud) and phase (which way) change together.

14Optical Link Power Budget

numerical
Q12 A fibre network uses single-mode fibre with attenuation 0.35 dB/km, a splitter with 14 dB loss, two mechanical splices of 0.1 dB each and two connectors of 0.75 dB each. Transmitter power is 5 dBm and receiver sensitivity −14 dBm. Calculate the maximum fibre length D between the OLT and the ONU.
Islamic Bank PLC SQA, Senior Officer (Network) · 14.03.2025
  1. Power budget = Ptx − Prx,min = 5 − (−14) = 19 dB
  2. Fixed losses = 14 + 2(0.1) + 2(0.75) = 14 + 0.2 + 1.5 = 15.7 dB
  3. Left for fibre = 19 − 15.7 = 3.3 dB
  4. D = 3.3 / 0.35 = ≈ 9.43 km
  5. Check: 9.43 × 0.35 + 15.7 = 19 dB → 5 − 19 = −14 dBm ✓

dBm − dBm = dB. If the paper also asks for a safety margin (often 3 dB for ageing and repairs), subtract it first: (3.3 − 3)/0.35 ≈ 0.86 km.

15Fibre Splicing Requirements

short
Q13 What are the main requirements of optical fibre splicing?
asked in a recruitment paper
Splice A permanent joint of two fibres: fusion (electric arc melts them together, ≈ 0.02–0.1 dB) or mechanical (aligned in a sleeve with index-matching gel, ≈ 0.1–0.3 dB). Connectors are the removable joints.
  1. clean, flat, 90° cleave of both ends
  2. precise core alignment (no lateral, angular or gap offset)
  3. matching fibres (same mode, core / mode-field diameter, NA)
  4. low insertion loss and low back-reflection
  1. clean ends: coating stripped, alcohol-wiped, no dust
  2. mechanical strength + protection (heat-shrink sleeve, splice tray)
  3. stable against temperature and humidity
  4. loss verified with an OTDR

16Millimetre Waves (mmWave) in IoT

asked 3×
Q14 What is the purpose and primary application of millimetre waves (mmWave) in IoT ecosystems?
Petrobangla AM (CSE) · 31.05.2024; BOGMC (Petrobangla) AM · 2024

mmWave = 30–300 GHz (λ = 10 mm to 1 mm); 5G FR2 uses ≈ 24–52 GHz (e.g. 26, 28, 39 GHz). Purpose: huge contiguous bandwidth (hundreds of MHz to GHz) → multi-Gbps rates, very low latency, many devices per area; tiny antennas allow massive-MIMO beamforming. Applications: 5G industrial IoT and smart factories (robots, machine vision), high-resolution radar sensing (60 GHz presence, 77 GHz automotive), fixed wireless access and backhaul, AR/VR and HD video surveillance, V2X. Limits: short range, line of sight, poor wall penetration, rain and oxygen absorption (60 GHz), so small cells are dense.

Memory trick mmWave = "more MHz, more trouble": big bandwidth and speed, but short reach and easily blocked.

18Viva Rapid-Fire

bank IT & BPSC viva

Q. What is a digital signal? Draw it. (BPSC viva 2023)
A signal with a limited set of discrete values (binary: two levels), changing only at bit boundaries; bit rate = 1 / bit interval.

Q. What is polarization?
The orientation of a wave's electric field: linear (H or V), circular (RHCP/LHCP) or elliptical. Antennas must match; satellites reuse one band twice with V and H.

Q. Where was optical fibre first used?
Glass-fibre bundles: medical endoscopes (1950s). Telecom: first live telephone traffic in 1977 (USA), on long trunk lines for low loss and high bandwidth.

Q. Satellite vs submarine cable?
Satellite: radio via a satellite, covers remote areas and islands, GEO latency ≈ 0.25 s per hop, rain fade. Cable: undersea fibre, Tbps capacity, low latency, fixed landing points, cuts need ship repair.

Q. Their uses?
Satellite: DTH TV, VSAT links for remote branches/ATMs, GPS, disaster backup, ships and aircraft. Cable: international Internet, voice, banking, cloud data.

Q. What is SEA-ME-WE?
South East Asia–Middle East–Western Europe: a consortium submarine fibre-optic cable system linking Asia, the Middle East and Europe.

Q. Landing stations in Bangladesh?
SMW-4: Cox's Bazar (2006) · SMW-5: Kuakata, Patuakhali (2017) · SMW-6: Cox's Bazar.

Exam trap SMW-5 lands at Kuakata, not Cox's Bazar; SMW-4 and SMW-6 are the Cox's Bazar cables.
