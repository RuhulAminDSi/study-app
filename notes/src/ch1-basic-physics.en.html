Basic Physics · Ch 1Electrostatics I

1Electric Charge & Coulomb's Law

MCQ + Written
Definition Charge (q): the property of matter that makes it feel an electric force. SI unit: coulomb (C) = 1 A·s. Two kinds: + and −.
Like charges repel, unlike attract. Check the sign first!
  • Quantised: q = ± n e, where e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
  • Conserved: total charge of an isolated system never changes
  • Invariant: same in every frame (unlike relativistic mass)
  • 1 C ≈ 6.25 × 10¹⁸ electrons
Coulomb's law
F = k q₁q₂ / r²
k = 1/4πε₀ = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²
ε₀ = 8.854 × 10⁻¹² C²/N·m²

In a medium: Fmedium = Fvacuum / εr → water (εr ≈ 80) cuts the force 80 times, which is why salt dissolves.

Exam trap Double r → F becomes ¼ (inverse square). Double both charges → 4F. Halve r → 4F.
Worked example ✍ Two 1 μC charges, 30 cm apart: F = 9×10⁹ × (10⁻⁶)² / (0.3)² = 9×10⁻³ / 0.09 = 0.1 N (repulsive)

2Electric Field (E)

Definition Force per unit positive test charge: E = F / q₀. A vector; unit N/C = V/m. Point charge: E = k q / r².
  • Lines start on +, end on −
  • They never cross (E has one direction per point)
  • Closer lines = stronger field
  • Meet a conductor at 90°; E = 0 inside it
Memory trick Field lines behave like water from a fountain: they flow out of + and drain into −. Analogy: gravity field g = F/m ↔ electric field E = F/q.

3Gauss's Law

high-yield
Statement Total electric flux through any closed surface = enclosed charge / ε₀:  ∮ E·dA = qenc / ε₀

Flux ΦE = EA cos θ (N·m²/C) = field lines piercing a surface.

Flux does NOT depend on the size or shape of the surface, only on q inside!

Charge outside the surface → net flux 0 (every line in also goes out).

Charge distributionField ERemember
Point charge / outside a spherekq/r²acts like a point charge
Infinite line (λ C/m)λ / 2πε₀r∝ 1/r
Infinite plane sheet (σ C/m²)σ / 2ε₀independent of distance
Just outside a conductorσ / ε₀twice the sheet value
Inside a conductor0charge sits on the surface
Classic MCQ Charge q at the centre of a cube → flux through one face = q / 6ε₀ (total q/ε₀ shared equally by 6 faces).

4Electric Potential (V)

Definition Work done per unit positive charge to bring it from infinity: V = W / q. A scalar; unit volt = J/C. Point charge: V = kq / r (∝ 1/r, not 1/r²)
  • E = −dV/dr: the field points from high to low potential
  • Energy: ΔU = qΔV; 1 eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J (energy of e through 1 V)
  • Equipotentials ⟂ field lines: no work along them; a conductor is one equipotential
Field EPotential V
typevectorscalar
point qkq/r²kq/r
unitN/CJ/C = V
Memory trick E is the downhill slope of V: a + charge rolls from high V to low V along E.
Worked example ✍ V at 10 cm from a 1 nC charge = 9×10⁹ × 10⁻⁹ / 0.1 = 90 V

Basic Physics · Ch 1Magnetism & Induction

5Magnetic Field (B)

Definition Region where a moving charge or current feels a force. Unit tesla (T) = N/(A·m); 1 T = 10⁴ gauss · μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A

Lorentz force: F = q(v × B)
|F| = qvB sin θ · on a wire: F = BIL sin θ

F ⟂ v → no work is done; speed stays fixed, the path is a circle with r = mv / qB.

SourceBNote
Long straight wireμ₀I / 2πr∝ 1/r
Centre of a circular loopμ₀I / 2RN turns → ×N
Long solenoid (n turns/m)μ₀ n Iuniform inside
Memory trick Fleming's left hand = motor: First finger = Field, seCond = Current, thuMb = Motion. Right hand = generator.
Exam trap Charge moving parallel to B (θ = 0°) or at rest → magnetic force = 0.

6Faraday's Law & Lenz's Law

high-yield
Faraday A changing magnetic flux induces an emf:  ε = −N dΦB/dt, where ΦB = BA cos θ (weber, Wb = T·m²)

Lenz's law (the minus sign): the induced current opposes the change that causes it. This is energy conservation.

Change B, area A or angle θ → emf. Rotating coil = AC generator: ε = NBAω sin ωt

Moving rod: ε = BLv (motional emf)

Uses: generator, transformer (Vs/Vp = Ns/Np, AC only), induction cooker, card readers, eddy-current brakes.

Worked example ✍ 100 turns, flux 0.02 Wb → 0 in 0.1 s: |ε| = 100 × 0.02 / 0.1 = 20 V

Basic Physics · Ch 1Maxwell & EM Waves

7Maxwell's Equations (1865)

high-yield

Four equations that unify electricity, magnetism and light:

#LawIntegral formMeaning
1Gauss (E)∮ E·dA = q / ε₀charges create E
2Gauss (B)∮ B·dA = 0no magnetic monopoles
3Faraday∮ E·dl = −dΦB/dtchanging B → E
4Ampère–Maxwell∮ B·dl = μ₀I + μ₀ε₀ dΦE/dtcurrent & changing E → B

Differential: ∇·E = ρ/ε₀ · ∇·B = 0 · ∇×E = −∂B/∂t · ∇×B = μ₀J + μ₀ε₀ ∂E/∂t

Displacement current Id = ε₀ dΦE/dt: Maxwell's own addition. It lets "current" flow across a charging capacitor's gap, and it is what makes EM waves possible.
Memory trick Order G-G-F-A: two divergences (sources: charge yes, monopole no) and two curls (circulation: Faraday, Ampère).

8EM Waves & the Spectrum

  • Maxwell predicted them; Hertz (1887–88) produced them
  • Speed c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀) = 3 × 10⁸ m/s
  • E ⟂ B ⟂ direction: transverse, E/B = c
  • Need no medium; carry energy & momentum
  • Microwave: Wi-Fi (2.4/5 GHz), mobile, radar, oven
  • Infrared: TV remote, thermal camera; optical fibre uses near-IR (1310/1550 nm)
  • UV: sterilisation, forged-note check
  • X-ray: medical imaging, crystal structure · Gamma: radiotherapy
Memory trick "Raging Martians Invaded Venus Using X-ray Guns" = increasing frequency.
Exam trap In vacuum every EM wave travels at the same c; they differ only in f and λ. Sound is not an EM wave.

9Oscillations & Simple Harmonic Motion

SHM Periodic motion in which the restoring force ∝ displacement and points back to the mean position: F = −kx ⇒ a = −ω²x

Frequency ff = 1/T (Hz)

Key equations

x = A sin(ωt + φ)

vmax = Aω (at mean position)

amax = Aω² (at the extremes)

Total energy E = ½kA² (constant)

KE max at mean, PE max at extremes

OscillatorPeriodDepends on
Spring–massT = 2π√(m/k)m, k (not g)
Simple pendulum (small θ)T = 2π√(L/g)L, g (not mass, not amplitude)

Worked example ✍ k = 100 N/m, m = 1 kg → ω = √(k/m) = 10 rad/s, T = 2π/10 ≈ 0.63 s
Exam trap Pendulum on the Moon (g/6) → T becomes √6 ≈ 2.45×. Double the bob's mass → no change. Length ×4 → T ×2.

Damped, forced & resonance

  • Damped: friction drains energy → amplitude decays (shock absorbers)
  • Forced: an external periodic force drives the system
  • Resonance: driving frequency = natural frequency → maximum amplitude (radio tuning, pushing a swing in step)
Memory trick Mean = max speed, Extreme = max acceleration. Like a swing: fastest at the bottom, stops and turns at the ends.

10Waves & the Wave Equation

Definition A wave is a travelling disturbance that carries energy, not matter.
TypeExamplesKey fact
Mechanicalsound, water, stringneed a medium
Electromagneticlight, radio, X-raytravel in vacuum
Transversestring, all EM wavescan be polarised
Longitudinalsound in aircannot be polarised
Wave formulas

v = f λ

k = 2π/λ (wave number), v = ω/k

y(x, t) = A sin(kx − ωt)

String: v = √(T/μ)

Wave equation: ∂²y/∂x² = (1/v²) ∂²y/∂t²; any shape f(x − vt) solves it.

  • Sound in air ≈ 343 m/s (20 °C; 332 at 0 °C)
  • vsolid > vliquid > vgas; no sound in vacuum
  • Constructive: path difference nλ; destructive: (n + ½)λ
  • Standing wave: node to node = λ/2
  • Beats: fbeat = |f₁ − f₂|
  • Doppler: source approaching → higher pitch

Audible range 20 Hz – 20 kHz; below = infrasonic (earthquakes), above = ultrasonic (SONAR, medical scans). Intensity ∝ A²: double the amplitude → 4× the energy.

Exam trap When a wave enters a new medium its frequency stays the same; only speed and λ change.
IT link ✍ Wi-Fi at 2.4 GHz: λ = c/f = 3×10⁸ / 2.4×10⁹ = 12.5 cm · FM 100 MHz: λ = 3 m → that is why antennas are sized in λ/2 or λ/4.

11Special Theory of Relativity

Einstein 1905
Postulates ① The laws of physics are the same in every inertial frame. ② The speed of light in vacuum, c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, is the same for all observers, whatever the source's motion.
Michelson–Morley (1887): no "ether" found, supporting postulate 2

Everything follows from the Lorentz factor: γ = 1 / √(1 − v²/c²)  (always ≥ 1)

EffectFormulaMeaning
Time dilationΔt = γ Δt₀moving clock runs slow
Length contractionL = L₀ / γonly along the motion
Relativistic massm = γ m₀→ ∞ as v → c
Mass–energyE = mc²rest energy m₀c²
Energy–momentumE² = (pc)² + (m₀c²)²photon: E = pc
v0.6c0.8c0.866c0.99c
γ1.251.6727.09
Muon proof ✍ Lifetime at rest 2.2 μs. At 0.99c: Δt = 7.09 × 2.2 ≈ 15.6 μs, so muons from the upper atmosphere reach the ground.
E = mc² ✍ 1 g of mass → E = 10⁻³ × (3×10⁸)² = 9 × 10¹³ J: the basis of nuclear power.
Exam trap Nothing with mass reaches c: u = (u′ + v)/(1 + u′v/c²) ⇒ c + anything = c. t₀, L₀ = proper values (rest frame). Charge never changes with speed.
Memory trick "Moving clocks run slow, moving rulers shrink": γ multiplies time, divides length.
IT link GPS satellite clocks need relativity corrections (≈ 38 μs/day net), or positions drift by km per day.

Basic Physics · Ch 1Quantum Theory of Light

12Quantum Theory of Light

Planck 1900 · Einstein 1905
Photon Light travels as packets (quanta) of energy E = hf = hc/λ, momentum p = h/λ = E/c, rest mass zero, speed c.

h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s · shortcut hc = 1240 eV·nm → E(eV) = 1240/λ(nm): red 620 nm = 2 eV

Wave–particle duality: interference & diffraction → wave; photoelectric effect & Compton effect → particle.

13Photoelectric Effect

high-yield
Definition Emission of electrons from a metal surface when light of high enough frequency falls on it. Observed by Hertz (1887); explained by Einstein (1905, Nobel 1921).
Einstein's equation  hf = φ + Kmax  ·  Kmax = eV₀ (V₀ = stopping potential) · threshold f₀ = φ/h, λ₀ = hc/φ
You increase…PhotocurrentKmax / V₀
Intensity (f fixed, f > f₀)increases (more photons)no change
Frequency (intensity fixed)≈ no changeincreases linearly
Anything, but f < f₀no emission, however bright · emission is instant
Exam trap Brighter light means more electrons, not faster ones. Classical wave theory fails on all three points: threshold, Kmax vs f, no time lag.
Worked example ✍ 400 nm light on sodium (φ = 2.3 eV): E = 1240/400 = 3.1 eV → Kmax = 3.1 − 2.3 = 0.8 eV → V₀ = 0.8 V

IT link: photodiodes in optical-fibre receivers, CCD/CMOS camera sensors and solar cells all work on photon absorption.

Basic Physics · Ch 1X-rays

14X-Rays

Röntgen 1895
Definition EM waves of λ ≈ 0.01 – 10 nm, produced when fast electrons strike a heavy-metal target (usually tungsten).

Two parts of the spectrum

  • Continuous (bremsstrahlung = "braking radiation"): electrons slowing down near nuclei
  • Characteristic lines (Kα, Kβ): inner-shell electron jumps → fingerprint of the target element

Properties

  • Penetrating & ionising; blacken photographic film
  • Not deflected by E or B fields (uncharged)
  • Hard X-rays = short λ = more penetrating
Cut-off wavelength  All of eV goes into one photon: λmin = hc / eV → λmin(nm) = 1240 / V(volts).  30 kV tube → λmin ≈ 0.041 nm

15X-Ray Diffraction & Bragg's Law

Atomic spacing in crystals (~0.1 nm) ≈ X-ray λ, so a crystal acts as a 3-D diffraction grating (Laue 1912; W.H. & W.L. Bragg 1913).

Bragg's law
2d sin θ = nλ (n = 1, 2, 3 …)

Uses: finding crystal structure; Rosalind Franklin's X-ray image (1952) revealed the DNA double helix.

Exam trap In Bragg's law θ is the glancing angle (ray to the crystal plane), not the angle to the normal. The largest possible λ is 2d (sin θ ≤ 1).
Worked example ✍ d = 0.28 nm, first order at θ = 15°: λ = 2 × 0.28 × sin 15° = 0.56 × 0.259 ≈ 0.145 nm

16Compton Effect

A.H. Compton 1923
Definition An X-ray photon scattered by a nearly free electron comes out with a longer wavelength. The photon behaves like a billiard ball with momentum p = h/λ.
Compton shift
Δλ = λ′ − λ = (h / mec)(1 − cos φ)
h / mec = 2.43 pm (Compton wavelength)
φ0°90°180°
Δλ02.43 pm4.85 pm (max)
  • Shift depends only on φ, not on λ or the target material
  • It proves photons carry momentum: strong evidence for the particle nature of light
Exam trap Why X-rays, not visible light? Δλ is only a few pm, which is negligible next to 500 nm but large next to 0.07 nm.

17de Broglie Waves (Matter Waves)

1924
Hypothesis If light (a wave) acts like particles, then particles act like waves:  λ = h / p = h / mv = h / √(2mK)
Electron through V volts
λ = 1.226 / √V nm
100 V → λ ≈ 0.123 nm, the same scale as X-rays → electrons diffract from crystals!
Why don't we see it? ✍ Cricket ball, 0.1 kg at 10 m/s: λ = 6.6×10⁻³⁴ / 1 = 6.6 × 10⁻³⁴ m, far too small to detect.
  • Davisson–Germer (1927): electron diffraction from a nickel crystal (54 V electrons) confirmed matter waves
  • Bohr orbits: 2πr = nλ (whole wavelengths fit) → quantised orbits
Memory trick Light → particle (photoelectric, Compton); particle → wave (de Broglie, Davisson–Germer). Same h links both.

IT link: electron microscopes use tiny electron λ to image chip features far smaller than visible light can resolve.

18Phase Velocity & Group Velocity

A localised particle = a wave packet, i.e. many waves of slightly different k added together.

Phase velocity vpGroup velocity vg
definitionω / k = E / pdω / dk = dE / dp
matter wavec² / v > c (!)= v (particle speed)
carriesno informationenergy & information

Product: vp · vg = c²
Light in vacuum: vp = vg = c
Heisenberg: Δx · Δp ≥ ħ/2, ħ = h/2π

Exam trap vp > c does not break relativity, because no signal travels at vp. Group velocity = dE/dp (some texts misprint dp/dm).

19Wave Function & Schrödinger Equation

1926
Wave function Ψ(x, t) Holds everything knowable about a particle. Ψ itself is not measurable and may be complex, but |Ψ|² = probability density (Born rule).
  • Normalised: ∫ |Ψ|² dx = 1
  • Single-valued, finite, continuous
  • dΨ/dx continuous (where V is finite)
  • Measurement "collapses" Ψ
Schrödinger equation

Time-dependent: iħ ∂Ψ/∂t = −(ħ²/2m) ∂²Ψ/∂x² + VΨ

Time-independent (steady states): −(ħ²/2m) d²ψ/dx² + Vψ = Eψ

Particle in a 1-D box (width L): En = n²h² / 8mL², n = 1, 2, 3 …

Energy is quantised; n = 1 is not zero (zero-point energy). IT link: quantum tunnelling programs and erases NAND flash cells (SSDs, pen drives).
