1Semiconductors in One Page

MCQ favourite
Definition A semiconductor has resistivity between a conductor and an insulator. Si and Ge are group-IV (4 valence electrons), held in covalent bonds. Band gap: Si 1.12 eV · Ge 0.67 eV · GaAs 1.42 eV. Resistance falls as temperature rises (negative temperature coefficient).

Intrinsic = pure crystal: n = p = ni (Si ≈ 1.5 × 10¹⁰ cm⁻³ at 300 K).

Extrinsic = doped (≈ 1 impurity atom per 10⁶–10⁸ Si atoms) → conductivity rises sharply.

Mass-action law: n · p = ni²

n-typep-type
Impuritypentavalent (donor)trivalent (acceptor)
ExamplesP, As, SbB, Al, Ga, In
Majorityelectronsholes
Minorityholeselectrons
Fixed ions+ donor ions− acceptor ions
Memory trick Five gives a spare electron → n-type ("PAS it on": P, As, Sb). Three takes one → a hole → p-type (B, Al, Ga, In).
Exam trap An n-type crystal is electrically neutral, not negative: every free electron left behind a fixed + donor ion.

2The PN Junction

high-yield

Diffusion of carriers across the junction leaves immobile ions → a depletion (space-charge) region, ≈ 0.5 µm wide.

Its field creates the barrier potential V₀: Si 0.7 V  Ge 0.3 V (25 °C).

V₀ falls ≈ 2 mV per °C rise.

Forward bias (p to +)Reverse bias (p to −)
Depletionbarrier lowered, region narrowsbarrier raised, region widens
Currentlarge (mA–A), majority carrierstiny IS (Si nA, Ge µA), minority carriers
Behaves likeclosed switch (after 0.7 V)open switch (until breakdown)
P to Positive = Forward

Reverse saturation current IS roughly doubles for every 10 °C rise. A BJT (next lesson) is simply two PN junctions back to back.

3Shockley Diode Equation

MCQ + Written
Equation ID = IS (eVD/ηVT − 1)   VT = kT/q ≈ 26 mV at 300 K
IS = reverse saturation current · η = ideality factor: 1 (Ge, IC silicon) to 2 (discrete Si at low current)
  • Forward, VD ≫ VT: ID ≈ IS eVD/ηVT (exponential)
  • Reverse, VD ≪ −VT: ID ≈ −IS (flat, tiny)
  • Cut-in (knee) voltage: Si 0.7 V, Ge 0.3 V: below it the current is negligible
  • Current ×10 needs only ΔV = ηVT ln 10 ≈ 60 mV (η = 1)
  • VD falls ≈ 2 mV/°C at constant current: diodes work as temperature sensors
Worked example ✍ IS = 10 fA, η = 1, VT = 26 mV. Find VD at 1 mA and at 10 mA.
VD = ηVT ln(ID/IS) = 0.026 ln(10¹¹) = 0.659 V at 1 mA; at 10 mA: 0.026 ln(10¹²) = 0.718 V (+60 mV per decade). This is why "0.7 V" works for any normal current.
Exam trap IS comes from minority carriers, so it depends on temperature, not on the reverse voltage. Ge has a far larger IS than Si: one reason Si replaced Ge.

4Reverse Breakdown: Zener vs Avalanche

high-yield
Zener breakdownAvalanche breakdown
Dopingheavy doping, very thin layerlight doping, wide layer
Mechanismfield emission: strong field (~10⁷ V/m) pulls electrons out of bondsimpact ionisation: carriers multiply by collisions
Voltagebelow ≈ 5 Vabove ≈ 6 V (up to kV)
Temp. coeff.negative (VZ falls as T rises)positive
Memory trick Zener = Zapped by the field (thin, low V, TC −). Avalanche = a snowball of collisions (wide, high V, TC +). At 5–6 V the two cancel → TC ≈ 0.
Practical link Breakdown is not destructive; heat (P = VI) is. 1N4001…1N4007: 1 A, PIV 50…1000 V; 1N4148: fast switching diode.

6Dynamic Resistance & Capacitance (AC model)

Static (DC): RD = VD/ID = 0.7 V/4.3 mA = 163 Ω.  Dynamic (AC): rd = ηVT/ID ≈ 26 mV/ID = slope at Q; at 4.3 mA rd = 6.0 Ω (+ bulk rB ≈ 0.1–2 Ω).

Exam trap rd falls as current rises (26 Ω at 1 mA, 2.6 Ω at 10 mA); small signals see rd, never RD.

7Load Line & Q-Point

KVL: VS = VD + IDR → a straight line on the I–V plane.

  1. ID = 0 → VD = VS (x-intercept)
  2. VD = 0 → ID = VS/R (y-intercept)
  3. Q-point = where the line cuts the diode curve

Larger R → flatter line.

8Zener Diode & Shunt Regulator

numericals
Definition Heavily doped diode made for reverse breakdown: VZ stays constant from IZK to IZM. In a regulator: reverse-biased, in parallel with the load.
Formulas
IS = (Vin − VZ)/R
IL = VZ/RL
IZ = IS − IL
PZ = VZ IZ · IZM = PZM/VZ
Zener ON only if VinRL/(R + RL) ≥ VZ
Load reg. = (VNL − VFL)/VFL × 100%
Worked example ✍ Vin = 20 V, VZ = 12 V, R = 400 Ω.
  1. No load: IZ = IS = (20 − 12)/400 = 20 mA (worst case: PZ = 240 mW)
  2. RL = 1.2 kΩ: IL = 12/1.2k = 10 mA → IZ = 20 − 10 = 10 mA, PZ = 120 mW
  3. Taking IZK ≈ 0: IL(max) = 20 mA → RL(min) = 12/0.02 = 600 Ω
Exam trap A forward-biased Zener is just a 0.7 V diode; it regulates only in reverse.

10Half-Wave Rectifier (HWR)

high-yield
HWR results
Vdc = Vm/π = 0.318 Vm · Vrms = Vm/2
Ripple factor γ = 1.21 · ripple frequency = f
ηmax = 4/π² ≈ 40.6% · PIV = Vm
Form factor 1.57 · peak factor 2 · TUF 0.287

Derivation: Vdc = (1/2π)∫₀π Vm sin θ dθ = Vm/π

γ = √((Vrms/Vdc)² − 1) = √(π²/4 − 1) = 1.21

η = Pdc/Pac = (Vm/π)²/(Vm/2)² = 4/π² = 0.405 (books print 40.6%)

Worked example ✍ 230 V, 50 Hz mains, 10 : 1 transformer, Si diode.
Vs = 23 V rms → Vm = 23√2 = 32.5 V
Vdc = (32.5 − 0.7)/π = 10.1 V (ideal 10.4 V)
PIV = 32.5 V · ripple at 50 Hz

CT results
2 diodes + centre-tapped transformer
Vdc = 2Vm/π = 0.637 Vm
γ = 0.48 · ηmax = 81.2% · ripple 2f
PIV = 2Vm · TUF 0.693
Exam trap In the CT circuit Vm is the peak of each half of the secondary. The OFF diode sees both halves in series → PIV = 2Vm (a frequently tested question).

12Bridge Full-Wave Rectifier

high-yield

+ half (left node +): D1 → RL → D2

− half (right node +): D3 → RL → D4

Load current is the same direction in both halves.

Two diodes in every path → drop 1.4 V (Si).

No centre tap; PIV only Vm; TUF 0.812.

Memory trick Double the waves, double the DC and the efficiency: 0.318 → 0.637, 40.6 → 81.2. Ripple "one-two-one" (1.21) vs "four-eight" (0.48). PIV: the centre tap is the odd one out (2Vm).
Exam trap HWR γ = 1.21 > 1: the AC ripple is larger than the DC. The exact values are 4/π² = 40.5% and 8/π² = 81.1%; MCQ options use 40.6% and 81.2%.
Worked example ✍ Bridge, 12 V rms secondary, Si diodes, RL = 100 Ω, 50 Hz.
  1. Vm = 12√2 = 16.97 V; load peak = 16.97 − 1.4 = 15.57 V
  2. Vdc = 2 × 15.57/π = 9.91 V (ideal 10.8 V) → Idc = 99 mA
  3. PIV ≈ Vm = 17 V → a 1N4001 (50 V, 1 A) is enough; ripple at 100 Hz

13Capacitor (Shunt) Filter

numericals
Idea A large C across RL charges to Vm at each peak and discharges slowly through RL → DC with a small sawtooth ripple.
Formulas (fr = ripple frequency)
Vr(p-p) ≈ Idc/(frC) = Vm/(frRLC)
Vdc ≈ Vm − Vr(p-p)/2
γ = 1/(2√3 frRLC)
HW: fr = f · FW: fr = 2f → γ = 1/(4√3 fRLC)
Worked example ✍ FW, 50 Hz, Vm = 15 V, RL = 500 Ω, C = 1000 µF.
Idc ≈ 15/500 = 30 mA
Vr(p-p) = 0.03/(100 × 0.001) = 0.30 V
Vdc ≈ 15 − 0.15 = 14.85 V
γ = 1/(4√3 × 50 × 500 × 10⁻³) ≈ 0.0058
Exam trap Half-wave + capacitor → PIV = 2Vm (C holds +Vm while the input is at −Vm).

More C or bigger RL → less ripple.

Other filters: series choke (heavy loads), LC (L-section), CLC π-filter (least ripple).

14Voltage Multipliers

  1. − half: D1 ON, C1 charges to Vm
  2. + half: source Vm + C1 Vm = 2Vm; D2 ON, C2 charges to 2Vm
  3. Each diode PIV = 2Vm; C2 rated 2Vm, C1 rated Vm
  4. Full-wave doubler: both C rated Vm, ripple 2f

Tripler 3Vm · quadrupler 4Vm · n stages nVm (Cockcroft–Walton). Small load currents only. Uses: CRT, X-ray, photocopier, laser printer.

Memory trick Doubler = clamper + peak rectifier. Each extra diode–capacitor pair adds one more Vm.

Electronics · Diode CircuitsWave shaping

15Clippers (Limiters)

high-yield
Definition Diode + resistor (no capacitor) that removes the part of a waveform above and/or below a level. Series: diode in series with the load; shunt: diode across it; biased: a battery sets the level.
  1. Find the input level where VAK = 0.7 V: here vin = VB + 0.7 = 2.7 V
  2. Above it: D ON → vo held at 2.7 V
  3. Below it: D OFF → vo = vin (to −10 V)

Double (two-level) clipper: two biased diodes in opposite directions → output limited between +(V1 + 0.7) and −(V2 + 0.7).

Back-to-back Zeners: ±(VZ + 0.7).

Exam trap In a series clipper the conducting half comes out as vin − 0.7 V, so a 10 V peak gives a 9.3 V output peak, not 10 V. Uses: protecting inputs, limiters in radio/TV, noise removal.

16Clampers (DC Restorers)

high-yield

C + diode (+ RL) shift the whole waveform up or down. Shape and peak-to-peak stay the same.

Needs RLC ≫ T/2 so C hardly discharges.

Worked example ✍ ±10 V square wave, 1 kHz, C = 1 µF, RL = 100 kΩ: τ = 100 ms ≫ T/2 = 0.5 ms ✓. Positive clamper, Si: −0.7 to +19.3 V; negative clamper: −19.3 to +0.7 V. p-p = 20 V in both.
Memory trick Clipper cuts, clamper climbs (shifts). Clamper has a Capacitor. Draw the diode from output to ground: the waveform moves the way its arrow points (arrow up → positive clamper).

Written question Draw a half-wave rectifier with its waveforms. Derive Vdc and Vrms, show that γ = 1.21 and ηmax ≈ 40.6%, and find Vdc for a 12 V rms secondary with a Si diode. 8 marks
  1. D conducts for 0 < θ < π: vo = Vm sin θ; vo = 0 for π < θ < 2π 1
  2. Vdc = (1/2π)∫₀π Vm sin θ dθ = (Vm/2π)[−cos θ]₀π = Vm/π 2
  3. Vrms = √[(1/2π)∫₀π Vm² sin²θ dθ] = √(Vm²/4) = Vm/2 2
  4. γ = √((Vrms/Vdc)² − 1) = √(π²/4 − 1) = 1.21; η = (Vm/π)²/(Vm/2)² = 4/π² ≈ 0.406 2
  5. Vm = 12√2 = 16.97 V; with 0.7 V drop: Vdc = 16.27/π = 5.18 V 1
