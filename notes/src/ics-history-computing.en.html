1What Is Computation?

MCQ + Written
Definition Computation is the transformation of input into output by a finite sequence of well-defined steps, i.e. by an algorithm. It can be carried out by a person, a mechanical device or an electronic computer. Until the 1940s "computer" was a job title for a person who did calculations.

Input → Algorithm → Output

  • Input: the data given (e.g. two numbers, a list of names)
  • Process: the rules / algorithm applied
  • Output: the result (sum, sorted list)
  • Storage keeps data between steps; this is the IPO(S) cycle of every computer

Algorithm: the five properties (Knuth)

PropertyMeaning
Finitenessmust stop after a finite number of steps
Definitenessevery step is precise and unambiguous
Inputzero or more inputs
Outputat least one output
Effectivenesseach step is basic enough to be done exactly, by pencil and paper
Memory trick FIDO-E: Finiteness, Input, Definiteness, Output, Effectiveness. "FIDO the dog is Effective."
Word origin "Algorithm" comes from al-Khwarizmi (c. 780–850, Baghdad), whose book al-jabr also gave us "algebra".
Worked example: Euclid's algorithm (c. 300 BCE) ✍ one of the oldest algorithms still in use. gcd(252, 105):
  1. 252 = 2 × 105 + 42 → gcd(105, 42)
  2. 105 = 2 × 42 + 21 → gcd(42, 21)
  3. 42 = 2 × 21 + 0 → stop: gcd = 21 (finite, definite, one output ✓)
Exam trap A program is an algorithm written in a programming language; a procedure that may run forever (e.g. an OS loop) is not an algorithm in the strict sense, because it lacks finiteness.

Church–Turing thesis: whatever is effectively computable is computable by a Turing machine.

Napier was Scottish, Oughtred English, Schickard and Leibniz German, Pascal, Jacquard and de Colmar French. The abacus survives as the Chinese suanpan and Japanese soroban. Pascal built the Pascaline at 19 to help his father, a tax official; the Antikythera gears (found in a shipwreck in 1901) predicted planet positions and eclipses.

Worked example ✍ 46 × 7 with Napier's bones: take the rods for 4 and 6, read row 7 → 2/8 and 4/2. Add along the diagonals from the right: units = 2; tens = 8 + 4 = 12 → write 2, carry 1; hundreds = 2 + 1 = 3 → 322 ✓
Memory trick 1642: the Pascaline appeared in the year Galileo died and Newton was born. Napier → Schickard → Pascal → Leibniz = 17, 23, 42, 73 (all 16xx).
Exam trap The Pascaline could only add and subtract (it subtracted with nines' complements). Leibniz's machine was the first to do all four operations. If an MCQ asks for the "first mechanical calculator" without listing Schickard, answer Pascaline.
IT link The programming language Pascal (Wirth, 1970) is named after Blaise Pascal; Leibniz also described the binary number system (1703), which every digital computer uses.

5Charles Babbage: the Engines

most asked
Who Charles Babbage (1791–1871), English mathematician: the father of the computer, because his Analytical Engine had every part of a modern computer.
Difference Engine
Proposed 1822. Tabulates polynomials by the method of finite differences: only addition. Special-purpose; unfinished. No. 2 was built by London's Science Museum in 1991: it works.
Analytical Engine
Designed 1834–37. General-purpose, programmable, steam-powered; punched cards (from Jacquard), loops, conditional branching; store of 1,000 40-digit numbers. Never built.
Worked example: method of differences ✍ f(x) = x² + x + 41
x012345
f(x)414347536171
Δ¹246810
Δ²2222

Degree 2 → Δ² is constant. Next Δ¹ = 8 + 2 = 10; next f = 61 + 10 = 71 (check: 25 + 5 + 41 = 71). Only two additions, no multiplication.

6Ada Lovelace: the First Programmer

Who Ada Lovelace (1815–1852), daughter of Lord Byron. In 1843 she translated Menabrea's paper on the engine and added Notes A–G; Note G, an algorithm for Bernoulli numbers, is the first published program. She foresaw computing with symbols and music.
Memory trick The Mill "mills" (processes) numbers; the Store stores them. The language Ada (US DoD, 1980) is MIL-STD-1815: her birth year.
Exam trap Babbage completed neither engine. The Analytical Engine is the first concept of a programmable computer, not a working one.

7Punched Cards: Hollerith to IBM

MCQ
Herman Hollerith built an electric tabulating machine that read punched cards for the 1890 US Census. The 1880 census took about 8 years to process; the 1890 count took about one year. This was the first large-scale data processing by machine.
  • Comptometer (Dorr Felt, 1887): first commercially successful key-driven calculator.
  • IBM's 80-column punched card (1928) became the standard; old terminals showed 80 characters per line for this reason.

Exam trap The abacus is digital (beads are discrete counts) while the slide rule is analog (a continuous scale). A thermometer and a car speedometer with a needle are analog.

ENIAC = decimal, Z3 & ABC = binary!
  • Z3 (Berlin, May 1941, ~2,600 relays) used binary floating point and read its program from punched film; destroyed in a 1943 air raid.
  • Colossus (Bletchley Park) broke the German Lorenz teleprinter cipher; it stayed secret until the 1970s.
  • Harvard Mark I = IBM ASCC: about 15 m long, read instructions from paper tape; Grace Hopper was one of its programmers.

ENIAC by the numbers

Programming ENIAC by setting switches and rewiring plugboards: a new problem took days. Its six first programmers were women. This weakness led directly to the stored-program idea (next sheet).
Exam traps ① ENIAC worked in decimal. ② Colossus broke Lorenz; Enigma was attacked with Turing's electromechanical Bombe. ③ ABC was not programmable; a 1973 US court voided the ENIAC patent, crediting Atanasoff.
Memory trick Alphabet = time order: Zuse (Z3, '41) → ABC ('42) → Colossus ('44) → ENIAC ('46). "Z-ACE".
IT link The first computer "bug": a moth stuck in a relay of the Harvard Mark II (9 Sept 1947), taped into the log book; Grace Hopper made "debugging" popular.

11The Stored-Program Idea

high-yield
Definition In a stored-program computer the instructions are kept in the same read-write memory as the data, so a new program is loaded, not wired. Described by John von Neumann in the First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC (June 1945), drawing on Eckert, Mauchly and Turing's universal machine.
  • binary representation
  • one memory for program + data
  • instructions fetched and executed one after another (fetch–decode–execute)
  • limitation: the von Neumann bottleneck (one path to memory)
Next lesson CPU, buses, von Neumann vs Harvard (separate program and data memory, as in the Mark I) → cs2, Hardware & Architecture.
  • UNIVAC I (Eckert–Mauchly, 1951): first commercial computer in the USA; first unit to the US Census Bureau; magnetic tape; predicted the 1952 election on TV.
  • Ferranti Mark 1 (UK, Feb 1951) came weeks earlier; LEO I (1951) ran the first business job.

Written question Describe Babbage's Analytical Engine, relate its parts to a modern computer, and explain why Ada Lovelace is called the first programmer. 8 marks
  1. What: designed 1834–37 by Charles Babbage; a general-purpose, programmable, steam-powered mechanical computer; never completed. 1
  2. Parts: Reader (punched cards, from Jacquard) = input; Mill = CPU/ALU; Store (1,000 × 40-digit numbers) = memory; barrel = control unit; printer = output. 3
  3. Why "computer": separate processor and memory, program on cards, loops and conditional branching: the logical design of a modern computer. 2
  4. Ada Lovelace: her 1843 Note G gave an algorithm for Bernoulli numbers on the engine, the first published program; she foresaw non-numeric computing. 2
