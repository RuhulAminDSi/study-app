1Oscillation & Simple Harmonic Motion

MCQ + Written
Definition Oscillation: a repetitive to-and-fro motion of a body about a fixed mean (equilibrium) position, repeating after a fixed time. Examples: pendulum, mass on a spring, tuning fork, AC current.

The five standard quantities

Amplitude A — max displacement

Period T — time of one oscillation

Frequency f — f = 1/T, unit hertz (Hz) = s⁻¹

Angular freq. ω — ω = 2πf = 2π/T

Phase φ — start position & direction

one full oscillation = 2π rad = 360°

Definition: SHM A periodic motion in which the restoring force is directly proportional to the displacement and directed toward the mean position: F = −kx. Since F = ma, a = −ω²x with ω² = k/m.
Memory trick "Mean = Max speed, Extreme = Max acceleration": at x = 0 speed is maximum and a = 0; at x = ±A the speed is zero and a is maximum.
Exam trap Amplitude A is a displacement, never the distance travelled: one oscillation covers 4A. Strictly SHM needs a constant amplitude, so a damped pendulum is not SHM.
Worked example ✍ A particle completes 20 oscillations in 8 s: f = 20/8 = 2.5 Hz, T = 0.4 s, ω = 2πf = 5π ≈ 15.7 rad s⁻¹. With amplitude 4 cm, the distance in 3 oscillations = 3 × 4A = 48 cm.
IT link Sampling an oscillating signal needs at least 2f samples per second (Nyquist), otherwise the frequency is aliased — a 50 Hz mains hum then looks like a low tone.

2Equations of Motion & Energy in SHM

high-yield
The three equations (derive them, don't memorise blind)

x = A sin(ωt + φ)    v = dx/dt = Aω cos(ωt + φ)    a = −ω²x = −Aω² sin(ωt + φ)

Eliminating t: v² = ω²(A² − x²) ⇒ v = ω√(A² − x²).

Energy At displacement x: KE = ½mv² = ½mω²(A² − x²), PE = ½kx² = ½mω²x². Adding: E = ½kA² = ½mω²A² — constant wherever the body is.
  • Energy is exchanged between KE and PE; ideal SHM loses none
  • KE and PE each reach their maximum twice per complete oscillation
  • Energy ∝ A²: double the amplitude ⇒ four times the energy

Memory trick Force is Restoring and Proportional — hence the minus sign. "KE at the Centre, PE at the Ends."
Exam trap At the extremes the speed is zero but the acceleration is maximum. ½kA² is the total energy; ½kA and kA² are both wrong.
Worked example ✍ A = 0.02 m, f = 5 Hz ⇒ ω = 10π ≈ 31.4 rad/s. vmax = Aω = 0.63 m s⁻¹; amax = Aω² = 19.7 m s⁻². At x = A/2, v = ω√(A² − x²) = 0.54 m s⁻¹.
x = A sin ωt only when x = 0 at t = 0
IT link A phone accelerometer is a tiny mass–spring oscillator: it measures a from x. A vibration tool picks the fundamental and its harmonics out of the FFT of the x–t trace.
SHM vs circular motion A body in uniform circular motion is not in SHM: its acceleration always points at the centre. Only its projection on a diameter is SHM.

3Two Systems You Must Know

written favourite
Mass on a spring Hooke's law F = −kx (k in N m⁻¹) ⇒ a = −(k/m)x ⇒ ω = √(k/m), T = 2π√(m/k). Independent of g and of amplitude.
Simple pendulum F = −mg sinθ ≈ −mgθ for small θ (< 10°), and x ≈ Lθ ⇒ a = −(g/L)x ⇒ ω = √(g/L), T = 2π√(L/g). Independent of the bob's mass. A larger amplitude makes T slightly longer: T ≈ T₀(1 + θ₀²/16), θ₀ in radians.

Worked example 1 ✍ m = 0.5 kg, k = 200 N/m ⇒ ω = √(200/0.5) = √400 = 20 rad/s; T = 2π/20 = 0.314 s; f ≈ 3.2 Hz.
Worked example 2 ✍ L = 1 m, g = 9.8 ⇒ T = 2π√(1/9.8) = 2.01 s. Make L four times ⇒ T = √4 × T = 2T, not 4T.
Memory trick Spring → watch k and m. Pendulum → watch L and g: the bob's mass cancels, so an m never appears in its T.
Exam trap L × 4 ⇒ T × 2, never × 4; on the Moon T × √6 ≈ 2.45. A spring–mass period has no g, so the Moon changes it not at all.
Worked example 3 ✍ Pendulum on the Moon (g = g/6): T = 2π√(L/(g/6)) = √6 × T ≈ 2.45 T — the same pendulum beats more slowly. The spring–mass above is unaffected.
Worked example 4 ✍ k = 800 N/m with m = 0.2 kg: f = (1/2π)√(800/0.2) = (1/2π)×√4000 = 10.1 Hz, T ≈ 0.099 s. Four masses in parallel → m = 0.8 kg → f falls by √4 = 2.
IT link A shock absorber is a deliberately damped mass on a spring; a radio tunes an LC oscillator, f = 1/2π√(LC) — the same resonance idea twice.

4Damped, Forced Oscillation & Resonance

high-yield
Damped oscillation A force opposite to the velocity (friction, air drag) leaks energy as heat: mx″ + bx′ + kx = 0, amplitude A = A₀e^(−bt/2m). The period is unchanged.
DampingWhere the energy goes
Viscous / airfluid friction → heat
Coulomb (dry)constant force, linear fall
Electromagneticeddy currents → Joule heat
Forced oscillation A periodic external force F₀ cos ωt drives the system: mx″ + bx′ + kx = F₀ cos ωt. After the transient dies out the body moves at the driving frequency, with amplitude A(ω) = (F₀/m) ⁄ √[(ω₀² − ω²)² + (bω/m)²].
Resonance The amplitude is maximum when the driving frequency equals the natural frequency: ω = ω₀ = √(k/m). There the force lags the displacement by π/2, so force is in phase with velocity and energy is pumped in fastest.

Sharpness: Q = ω₀m/b. Large Q (little damping) ⇒ tall narrow peak; small Q ⇒ broad flat curve. Used in radio/TV tuning, the resonance column, MRI and organ pipes. Danger: bridge resonance, marching soldiers, tall buildings in an earthquake.

Memory trick "Damaged = amplitude dies, period lives." Resonant peak ⇔ energy pumped in per cycle equals energy lost per cycle.
Exam trap Resonance means matching frequencies, not "always loud"; a big amplitude needs low damping. With damping the amplitude peak sits slightly below f₀.
Worked example ✍ ω₀ = 20 rad/s. Driven at 20 rad/s ⇒ resonance. At 40 rad/s, with negligible damping A ∝ 1/(ω₀² − ω²), so A ∝ 1/(ω₀² − 4ω₀²) ⇒ the amplitude is one third of the resonant value.
Worked example ✍ Sharpness: ω₀ = 20 rad/s, m = 0.5 kg, b = 2 kg s⁻¹ ⇒ Q = ω₀m/b = 20 × 0.5/2 = 5. Halve b and Q doubles: the peak becomes twice as tall and half as wide.

5Wave Motion & the Wave Equation

high-yield
Definition A wave is a travelling disturbance that transfers energy without transferring matter: the particles of the medium oscillate about their own mean positions while the pattern moves on. Sound crosses air at 340 m s⁻¹ yet no air molecule travels that far.
TypeExamplesKey factTypeExamplesKey fact
Mechanicalsound, water, stringneeds a mediumTransversestring, light, surfacecan be polarised
Electromagneticlight, radio, X-raytravels in vacuumLongitudinalsound in air, springnever polarised
The wave equation and its symbols

y(x, t) = A sin(kx − ωt + φ)  (A cos is equally valid; the sign only fixes the direction of travel)

A = amplitude (m)

λ = wavelength (m)

k = 2π/λ = wave number (rad m⁻¹)

ω = 2πf = angular frequency

v = fλ = ω/k

∂²y/∂x² = (1/v²)∂²y/∂t²

Δφ = 2πΔx/λ = 2πfΔt

string: v = √(F/μ)

Speed of a wave String v = √(F/μ) · fluid v = √(B/ρ) · solid rod v = √(Y/ρ). Order vsolid > vliquid > vgas; sound in air ≈ 331 m/s (0 °C), 343 m/s (20 °C); EM waves in vacuum 3 × 10⁸ m/s.
Exam trap A wave crossing into a new medium keeps the same frequency — only v and λ change (v ∝ λ). Energy ∝ A²: double A ⇒ 4× the power. Sound is mechanical: no sound in vacuum.
Memory trick "Go From a Crest to the next Crest" and λ is done — one full oscillation of every point of the medium. k = 2π/λ is a wave number in rad m⁻¹, not a frequency.
Exam trap A wave is not matter moving: the amplitude of the medium particles is not the amplitude of the wave. Wave and particle speeds are unrelated — a long wavelength can travel fast.
Worked example ✍ f = 500 Hz, λ = 0.68 m ⇒ v = fλ = 500 × 0.68 = 340 m s⁻¹. In water (v = 1500 m s⁻¹) the frequency stays 500 Hz, so λ = v/f = 3 m. · Wi-Fi 2.4 GHz: λ = c/f = 3×10⁸/2.4×10⁹ = 12.5 cm — routers use λ/2 or λ/4 antennas.

6Superposition, Interference & Beats

high-yield
Principle of superposition When waves meet, the resultant displacement is the algebraic sum of the individual displacements. The waves pass through each other unchanged and continue on their way — the basis of radio interference, noise cancellation and beam forming.
CasePath difference ΔPhase differenceResult
Constructive (in phase)nλ (n = 0, 1, 2 …)2nπamplitude maximum
Destructive (out of phase)(n + ½)λ(2n + 1)πamplitude minimum — zero if A₁ = A₂
Beats Two waves of equal amplitude and very nearly equal frequency superpose: the sum is a fast vibration inside a slow swelling and shrinking envelope, heard as loud → soft → loud.

y = 2A cos[π(f₁ − f₂)t] · cos[π(f₁ + f₂)t]  ⇒  fbeat = |f₁ − f₂|, interval between beats = 1 ⁄ fbeat.

  • Beats are distinct only for a small difference (roughly < 20 Hz); f₁ = f₂ ⇒ no beats, one steady tone
  • Uses: detecting an inaudible frequency by beating it with a known one, calibrating a tuning fork, tuning by beat notes
Memory trick The beat frequency is the difference; the note you hear is the average: fnote = (f₁ + f₂)/2, fbeat = |f₁ − f₂|.
Exam trap fbeat is not the average and not a signed difference — always take the absolute value. If |f₁ − f₂| > 20 Hz you only hear a change in quality.
Worked example ✍ Forks of 440 Hz and 444 Hz: fbeat = 4 Hz, interval 0.25 s, 40 beats in 10 s, average pitch 442 Hz. To test whether an unknown tone is 1000 or 1004 Hz, sound it with a 1000 Hz fork: 4 beats s⁻¹ ⇒ it is the 1004 Hz one.
IT link Two nearby Wi-Fi access points on the same channel beat against each other (interference → dropouts); superheterodyne radios deliberately mix two frequencies and listen to the difference (beat) frequency.

7Standing Waves, Nodes & Harmonics

written favourite
Standing wave Two identical waves travelling in opposite directions superpose: A sin(kx − ωt) + A sin(kx + ωt) = 2A sin kx cos ωt. The pattern no longer travels — it stands still, so no energy is transferred along it.
PointDisplacementAmplitude
Nodealways zero0
Antinodeoscillates fullymax = 2A
Node → node—λ/2
Node → next antinode—λ/4
String, both ends fixed Both ends are nodes: L = nλ/2 ⇒ fn = nv/2L, n = 1, 2, 3 … all harmonics. Nodes = n + 1, antinodes = n.
Pipe open at both ends Both ends are displacement antinodes: L = nλ/2 ⇒ fn = nv/2L, all harmonics. Antinodes = n, nodes = n − 1.
Pipe closed at one end Closed end = node, open end = antinode: L = (2n − 1)λ/4 ⇒ fn = (2n − 1)v/4L ⇒ odd harmonics only (f, 3f, 5f).

Memory trick "Open = all Orders allowed (1, 2, 3); Closed = Count only the odds (1, 3, 5)." So a closed pipe's first overtone needs 3L, not 2L.
Exam trap Node to node is λ/2, not λ. Both ends of a fixed string are nodes; both ends of an open pipe are antinodes. Standing waves carry no net energy along the medium.
Worked example ✍ String L = 2 m, μ = 0.5 kg m⁻¹, F = 200 N ⇒ v = √(F/μ) = √400 = 20 m s⁻¹; f₁ = v/2L = 5 Hz; f₃ = 3f₁ = 15 Hz (λ₃ = 2L/3 = 1.33 m, 4 nodes, 3 antinodes). Closed pipe L = 0.25 m, v = 340 ⇒ f₁ = v/4L = 340 Hz, first overtone 1020 Hz.

8The Doppler Effect

high-yield
Definition The apparent change in frequency (and hence in pitch and observed wavelength) of a wave when there is relative motion between source and observer. Christian Doppler, 1842.
MotionObserved frequency f′MotionObserved frequency f′
Source approaching (> f)f′ = f v/(v − vs)Observer approaching (> f)f′ = f(v + vo)/v
Source receding (< f)f′ = f v/(v + vs)Observer receding (< f)f′ = f(v − vo)/v
Both movingf′ = f(v + vo)/(v − vs)Light (relativistic)f′ = f√[(1+β)/(1−β)], β = v/c

Here v is the speed of the wave in that medium (340 m s⁻¹ for sound in air) and the medium is taken to be at rest. What matters is the compression of the wavelength, so a source and an observer moving together with the medium hear nothing unusual.

  • Sound: a siren's pitch rises then falls as it passes
  • Echo / radar: the shift is applied twice, out and back — fecho = f(v + vo)/(v − vo); police radar guns, Doppler ultrasound
  • Blood flow: cells moving towards the probe shift the echo up, and the shift measures their speed
  • Light: galaxy receding ⇒ redshift (expanding universe); approaching ⇒ blueshift
Memory trick "Crush in ⇒ Crowd": closer wavefronts, higher frequency. "Flee far ⇒ Flat." Whatever the cause — approach ⇒ high, recede ⇒ low.
Exam trap Only the wave speed in the medium appears, not the source–observer relative speed. Doppler changes pitch, not loudness. For light use f′ = f√[(1+β)/(1−β)].
Worked example ✍ A 1000 Hz siren at 15 m s⁻¹ (v = 340): approaching f′ = 1000 × 340/325 = 1046 Hz; receding f′ = 340000/355 = 958 Hz. Observer at 20 m s⁻¹ towards a still source: f′ = 1000(340 + 20)/340 = 1059 Hz.
