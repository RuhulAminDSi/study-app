### Continuation of an earlier paper (unidentified) — JFET answer, cut off

When a voltage is applied between the source and drain, a current flows through the channel. However, when a voltage is applied between the gate and source, an electric field is created that controls the width of the channel and, therefore, the current flow between the source and drain.

The JFET has a characteristic curve that shows the relationship between the gate-source voltage (Vgs) and the drain-source current (Ids). This curve can be divided into three regions of operation: cutoff, triode (or linear), and saturation.

- **Cutoff:** When Vgs is less than the threshold voltage (Vth), the JFET is in cutoff and there is no current flow between the source and drain. The channel is completely depleted, and the JFET acts like an open circuit.
