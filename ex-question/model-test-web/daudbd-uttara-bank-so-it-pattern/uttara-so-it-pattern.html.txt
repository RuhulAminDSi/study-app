Overview of the Written Examination
The written examination for Uttara Bank Senior Officer (IT) typically contains both general skill sections and technical IT sections. While the bank does not officially publish a fixed syllabus, past exam experience shows a consistent pattern. The test is usually conducted in a MCQ (Multiple Choice) format with a time limit ranging from 60 to 90 minutes. The exam is designed to judge analytical ability, ICT knowledge, as well as practical IT problem-solving skills that are essential for daily banking operations.

Main Sections of the Exam
The exam is generally divided into several major parts. Each part focuses on a different skill set relevant to banking operations and IT management. Below is the most commonly observed structure:
1. English Language & Communication
This section checks a candidate’s ability to understand bank-level communication. Expect questions on grammar, sentence correction, synonyms–antonyms, reading comprehension, and error spotting. The difficulty is moderate, but accuracy matters because this section helps evaluate workplace communication skills. Candidates often face short passages followed by 3–4 questions.
2. General Mathematics & Logical Reasoning
This part focuses on quantitative aptitude, pattern analysis, number series, ratios, percentages, probability, and logical puzzle-type reasoning questions. The time pressure is high, so candidates must be quick with mental math and basic formulas. Logical reasoning questions (seating arrangement, coding-decoding, analogy, and direction problems) appear frequently.

3. General Knowledge & Banking Awareness
This portion tests the candidate’s awareness of the financial sector. Questions usually relate to Bangladesh Bank policies, banking terms, digital banking, ICT Act, cybercrime laws, cloud adoption in banks, national IT infrastructure, and current economic trends. Also expect questions about Government ICT projects, digital transformation, mobile banking systems, and fintech products used in Bangladesh.

4. Core IT & Technical Knowledge
This is the most important section of the exam. It evaluates practical and theoretical knowledge in IT. The topics generally include:
  Computer networks (IPv4, IPv6, routing, switching, OSI layers)
  Database systems (SQL queries, indexing, normalization)
  Operating systems (Linux, Windows Server, process management)
  Programming concepts (OOP, data structures, pseudocode logic)
  Cybersecurity & cryptography (firewalls, hashing, encryption)
  System design, backup strategy, disaster recovery
  IT governance (COBIT, ITIL, audit controls)
Questions are usually scenario-based, requiring candidates to choose the best solution among four options. Some questions test practical experience, such as identifying correct SQL outputs or choosing the correct network configuration.

Time Management Tips for the Exam
Because the test is highly competitive, speed and accuracy are crucial. Attempt easy English, GK, and reasoning questions first. Save the IT section for after you build confidence, since it requires deeper thinking. Avoid getting stuck on long numerical or scenario-based questions. Use elimination technique—remove obviously wrong options to increase accuracy. Remember that IT carries more weight in selection, so allocate at least 45–50% of time to this part.
